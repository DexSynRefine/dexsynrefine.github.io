DexSynRefine: Synthesizing and Refining Human-Object Interaction Motion for Physically Feasible Dexterous Robot Actions

Anonymous Authors
Paper Video

TL;DR: Synthesizing sparse HOI motions as pose-conditioned references and refining them into physically feasible dexterous robot actions via task-space residual RL.

Abstract

Human–object interaction (HOI) recordings capture coordinated hand and object motion without robot teleoperation, but a few demonstrations cover only a narrow range of initial states and omit the contact dynamics that robot execution requires. We present DexSynRefine, which separates reference synthesis from physical refinement. From seven recordings per task, object-centric augmentation and a conditional motion-manifold flow prior (HOI-MMFP) synthesize coupled wrist, hand, and object references for new initial object poses. Task-space residual reinforcement learning then corrects the reference wrist and fingertip targets in simulation, and a student policy recovers the required contact and dynamics context from proprioceptive history for deployment. Across five dexterous tasks, HOI-MMFP references yield higher average downstream success than retrieved demonstrations (68.1% vs. 46.6%) and a smaller gain over full-trajectory generation (63.1%), task-space residuals exceed the next-best action representation by 16.8 points, and combining contact and dynamics context is critical on the two most contact-rich tasks. On a 7-DoF arm with a 16-DoF hand, the deployed student succeeds in 34/50 trials compared with 5/50 for kinematic retargeting, and in 15/20 matched-pose trials compared with 11/20 for the best alternative adaptation baselines. These results indicate that synthesized HOI motion should be grounded through robot-specific residual refinement rather than executed directly.

HOI-MMFP: HOI Motion Manifold Flow Primitives

HOI-MMFP is a generative motion prior for coupled wrist–hand–object motion. From seven recordings per task, object-centric augmentation produces about 300 trajectories. A Transformer autoencoder compresses each coupled wrist–hand–object trajectory into a 16-D motion manifold, and conditional latent flow matching generates a new latent from the task embedding and the initial object pose. The frozen decoder turns it into a synthetic HOI reference.

HOI Trajectory Synthesis Results

HOI-MMFP preserves the canonical start pose with the lowest first-wrist-pose error (0.015 m, 2.50°) and produces smooth, task-consistent motion. Used as references for the same residual-RL teacher, HOI-MMFP reaches 68.1% average success, versus 63.1% for DiT-Full (flow matching in full trajectory space), 46.6% for nearest-demonstration retrieval with rigid alignment, and 22.6% for raw augmented trajectories. The margin over DiT-Full is a modest 5.0 points on average and largest on Hammer (60.3% vs. 49.1%).

Task-Space Residual Refinement

A privileged teacher learns bounded task-space residuals that correct the wrist and fingertip targets of the synthesized reference while preserving its interaction structure. A deployable student, initialized from the teacher, replaces the privileged contact and dynamics inputs with estimates from proprioceptive history, so no simulator-only state is needed on the real robot.

Refinement Results in Simulation

With the HOI-MMFP reference fixed, task-space residual actions reach 68.1% average success, 16.8 points above the next-best ObjectCentricDex action representation (51.3%) and ahead of joint-space absolute (34.4%), joint-space residual (29.0%), and task-space absolute (8.3%) actions. Executing the reference through kinematic retargeting alone reaches 1.6%. Success rates below are for the privileged teacher.

Contact Adaptation

The student estimates binary hand contacts and contact forces from its proprioceptive history. On the two most contact-rich tasks, removing either the contact or the dynamics context sharply reduces success; with both, the Watering Can student nearly matches its privileged teacher (51.8% vs. 52.4%).
