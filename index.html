DexSynRefine: Synthesizing and Refining Human-Object Interaction Motion for Physically Feasible Dexterous Robot Actions

Anonymous Authors
Paper Video

TL;DR: Synthesizing sparse HOI motions and refining them into physically feasible dexterous robot actions via downstream task-space residual RL.

Abstract

Human–object interaction (HOI) recordings capture coordinated hand and object motion without robot teleoperation, but a few demonstrations cover only a narrow range of initial states and omit the contact dynamics that robot execution requires. We present DexSynRefine, which separates reference synthesis from physical refinement. From seven recordings per task, object-centric augmentation and a conditional motion-manifold flow prior (HOI-MMFP) synthesize coupled wrist, hand, and object references for new initial object poses. Task-space residual reinforcement learning then corrects the reference wrist and fingertip targets in simulation, and a student policy recovers the required contact and dynamics context from proprioceptive history for deployment. Across five dexterous tasks, HOI-MMFP references yield higher downstream success than retrieved demonstrations or full-trajectory generation (68.1% vs. 46.6% and 63.1%), task-space residuals outperform alternative action representations, and combining contact and dynamics context is critical on the two most contact-rich tasks. On a 7-DoF arm with a 16-DoF hand, the deployed student succeeds in 34/50 trials compared with 5/50 for kinematic retargeting, and in 15/20 matched-pose trials compared with 11/20 for the best alternative adaptation baselines. These results indicate that synthesized HOI motion should be grounded through robot-specific residual refinement rather than executed directly.

Synthesizing trajectories directly in trajectory space produces jittery and jerky motions, while our manifold-based synthesis yields smooth, physically plausible HOI trajectories.

Task-Space Residual Action Learning

A privileged teacher learns bounded task-space residuals that correct the wrist and fingertip targets of the synthesized reference while preserving its interaction structure. A deployable student then recovers the contact and dynamics context from proprioceptive history, so no privileged simulation state is needed on the real robot.
